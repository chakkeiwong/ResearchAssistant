## Review History

Round 1 found that the original planning-only phase required documentation,
implementation, commit/install, and packet artifacts that it simultaneously
forbade creating. It also found an overbroad universal zero-secret-persistence
claim and ambiguous ownership of the identified successor commit.

The repair split M20B into:

- M20B0 decision/setup planning;
- M20B1 official authentication/pricing documentation;
- M20B2 synthetic-canary credential/redaction/cost implementation;
- M20B3 identified commit/install and exact packet freeze; and
- M20B4 one exact live attempt after final human authorization.

Round 2 found that fresh human approval for the bounded public-document fetch
was disproportionate under current repository policy and that prelaunch
“hashes” were ambiguous. The repair now uses the existing execute/resume
direction plus trusted platform/network permission for M20B1. Prelaunch hashes
are limited to artifacts that exist before acquisition; response-body and
completed-manifest hashes are post-acquisition evidence.

## Final Finding

No material finding remains. Human decisions remain explicit before M20B2 for
the key interface, privacy handling, and numeric cost/credit budget, and before
M20B4 for the exact final packet. Phase ownership, bounded canary claims,
M20A-local versus M20-milestone status, and the non-executable M20B1 draft are
consistent.

This agreement does not authorize a credential, implementation, cost, provider
API call, source/PDF/full-text access, M21, push, release, or scientific/product
claim. The bounded M20B1 documentation command still requires exact local
packet completion and trusted platform/network permission.
